[ Project ]  Mochi

Mochi

A baby tracker for the two of you. Feeds, diapers, sleep and pumping in one tap, a log that lives on your phone, and an app that learns your baby’s rhythm from it — which side is next, when the next feed and nap are likely.

Called Coo until November 2026.

Get it on Google Play → The Mochi page & film → Privacy policy → On Google Play — Android, in seven languages. My own family uses it every day. Feedback is very welcome: andrzej@lechdigital.nl.
[ 01 ]  Why

The briefing at 3 a.m.

I have a baby of my own, and for a while the log of our days was a piece of paper. My girlfriend and I wrote down every feed and every diaper on it — or I typed them into the notes on my phone, and then gave her the briefing at three in the morning.

I wanted an app to replace the paper. I could not find one on the Play Store that had all of it in one place, for free. So I built it.

The part I built it for is free, and stays free: logging feeds, diapers, sleep and pumping, the stats and the patterns, and sharing that log with your family. Premium is for what costs me money to run or takes extra care — personal nap times, a nap reminder, more logging by voice and more Advisor questions, and a photo of your baby as the avatar — for a month, a year, or once and forever.

[ 02 ]  What it does

One tap, then back to the baby

Feeds: breastfeeding with a timer per side and a switch between them, or a bottle of formula or expressed milk, in ml or oz. Diapers: wet or dirty. Sleep: naps and night sleep, with a timer. Pumping: a timer and the amount.

The Today screen is the briefing, done for you: how long the baby has been awake or asleep, and the last feed, diaper, sleep and pump at a glance, with quick-log tiles and the day’s timeline underneath. The Log screen draws each day as a rhythm band — sleep filled in, feeds as long as they took, diapers and pumps marked, quiet hours shaded — and a tap opens it zoomed to six hours with every item labelled.

During your quiet hours (22:00 to 07:00 unless you change them), or whenever the phone is in dark mode, it switches to a dark night palette with big buttons, built for one hand in the dark. Anything can be edited, logged after the fact or deleted with an undo, and if an entry looks like a duplicate or isn’t plausible, Mochi asks first. It speaks English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Polish and Dutch.

Both parents sign in with their own Google account and share one log between their phones — the second is invited by a link, and sync keeps working offline. A nanny or daycare can log too, and grandparents can follow along without changing anything. Home-screen widgets and a timer in the notification bar let you log without opening the app.

There is more than feeds and sleep: health and growth — weight, length and head on the WHO growth charts, medicines and vitamins, temperatures, vaccinations and doctor visits — and a PDF report to take to the paediatrician. Reminders for the next feed, the diaper check, the bath and each medicine can be answered straight from the notification. And the firsts: tummy time, solid food with any reaction, and milestones from the first smile to the first steps.

[ 03 ]  What’s interesting

It learns her rhythm — on the phone

The log is more than a record. From the baby’s own entries Mochi works out which side to offer next, when the next feed is likely (night feeds are further apart, and it knows her evening cluster feeding), when the next nap is due from her wake windows, and at night when she is likely back asleep from her own wakings. Stats draws her sleep pattern day by day, noon to noon, and trends by day, week or month.

None of it is a model in the cloud: it is plain statistics, weighted by how alike the moments are, running on the phone. It says “likely 12:37–1:07 PM”, never a precise time it doesn’t believe, and “Why this time?” shows the reasoning.

The phone is the source of truth

A log about a child is health data, so it stays home. Mochi works fully offline, needs no account until you share, keeps the log on the phone, and carries no ads, analytics or trackers. Android backup is switched off for it on purpose.

When you share, the server is how phones catch up with each other, not the place the data lives. The sync protocol is built around that: an outbox of local changes, hybrid logical clocks to order edits made on different phones, and per-household sequence numbers so a phone asks only for what it missed. It runs on my own server at Hetzner in Nuremberg — no Firebase.

The app is .NET 10 MAUI around a pure, hexagonal domain model. Every increment is specified and agreed before any code, and verified on a real Pixel 10 before it counts as done, with more than 2,700 automated tests underneath.
